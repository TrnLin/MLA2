# Audience gaps: snapshot review

This audit compares teacher **development** gender × articleType × Usage with inferred intended product audience. It does not infer a person's gender from a photo and never writes a gender training target. Unknown means the evidence did not establish audience; it does not mean the product excludes any audience. A neutral bag, pillow or watch is not automatically Unisex.

`audience_gap_table.csv` lists each teacher subgroup with existing, **advisory retained-after-128-shortlist**, and proposed replacement coverage. The advisory retained counts are not final v3 counts. The actual current selection plan is130: Smart62, Party44, Travel17, Home7. `external_audience_annotations.csv` records the exact source basis for every annotation. `audience_snapshot_inputs.json` saves hashes and row counts of selected input files. Run `audience_review.py` again after any later substitutions.

## Clear gaps

- **Smart Women, refreshed after substitution:** teacher9 =4 shirts,4 watches,1 sandal. Existing162 external have155 Men and7 Women by source evidence; all7 Women's items are footwear. The refreshed62 selected rows have17 Women,33 Men,9 Unknown,3 conflicting/broad audience tags. Same-audience support now includes6 blouses/shirts,8 watches and3 sandals. The six blouse IDs are verified members of AAIKO's explicitly women's collection; watches use product prose or exact product audience tags. One of the intended eight new women's watches (Fossil Harlow ES5480) has simultaneously Men/Women/Ladies/Unisex tags, so it remains conflicting rather than forced to Women. The final17 supplemental rows contribute16 Women (six blouses, seven watches and three sandals) plus one conflicting watch (Fossil Harlow ES5480). The eight counted Women's watches across all62 include the earlier Casio Classic Analogue MTP1302PD6AVEF, whose captured retailer audience tag says Women; that source-only annotation remains provisional. These counts show improved subgroup support, not a fully solved gap.
- **Party:** all12 teacher development examples are Women. The selected44 snapshot contains three explicitly Man perfumes, one Unisex perfume, and three watches marketed Girls. These can have supported Party use, but their audience support differs from the teacher subgroup. Prioritize women's-source evidence when replacing them; do not relabel Girls as Women automatically. Most clutch and dress audience fields remain Unknown because their captured text lacks an explicit audience. Their visual form alone does not establish an audience label.
- **Travel:** valid visual groups are Unisex18 and Women3 (two outdoor handbags and one pink backpack). The only Men row is the noisy folded-shirt ID12348 and is excluded as a sourcing target. Existing external Travel is largely audience-unknown. A camera-bag intake addresses product form, not a demonstrated Unisex label. The pink women's backpack and structured women's outdoor-handbag subgroup remain thinly supported.
- **Home:** the single teacher row is Unisex, but home-textile titles need no gender designation. External Unknown audience is not an actionable gap here. The useful change is set presentation and printed covers; no gender-specific Home quota is justified.

## Other remaining limits

All four teacher women's Smart shirts have baseColour White; three are related Scullers striped-shirt examples and occupy only folds1/2. The refreshed women's contact sheet was inspected: its six blouses are mostly brown/dark or green, isolated packshots; teacher shirts are white/light and modelled. This colour/presentation gap remains. Do not turn four correlated images into a fixed colour quota. The women's watches include black/white dial metal bracelets and a blue Casio Baby-G. The new small-case metal/strap dress-watch designs improve variety, but do not match every teacher shape. Require actual product/collection evidence; do not relabel a sports-style watch by appearance.

Teacher Party is visually mostly dark/black catalogue clothing plus metallic accessories. New dress photographs are often bright gowns/lifestyle scenes. Teacher baseColour describes different things across types (for example, dial colour for a watch rather than its large silver bracelet), so a title's colour word is not a comparable image-colour statistic. No exact colour quotas are justified. `teacher_audience_colour_counts.csv` preserves the raw teacher counts for context.

The main evidence limits remain: retailer Evening/Cocktail/Night Out tags are proxies for Party; Smart Casual collection or styling prose may span Formal/Casual boundaries; travel can be one of several stated uses. Audience coverage does not fix these Usage ambiguities. Family/source variety and the frozen folds still matter; no subgroup is marked fully solved.

M.J. Bale shirt audience uses an explicitly marked **category-level inference**, supported by the official men's-shirt category title and description saved in `mjbale_shirts_collection.html`. Exact individual membership in that rendered category was not verified. Other annotations prefer explicit product title, then captured product/collection text, then explicit product-URL wording. For unresolved selected rows, exact product tags are read from the named evidence JSON; AAIKO membership is verified by product ID. Broad tags that include all audiences remain conflicting. Generic brand names and perceived model gender are not used.
