01
The brief

The graded artefact is the reasoning, not the accuracy

What the specification asks for, and the one sentence in it that should shape every decision you make.

Fashion e-commerce platforms use machine learning to organise and search catalogues far too large to tag by hand. Given a product photograph, the system should identify the item, infer who and when it is for, and surface visually similar products. That is the problem this assignment simulates, using a catalogue of roughly 38.6 thousand training images and 5.8 thousand unlabelled test images.

The deliverables are four: a report of no more than five pages plus two pages of appendices; at least four trained models with the scripts that run them; a set of predictions produced by your ultimate judgement; and the notebooks or scripts that built everything, with a README so a marker can reproduce it on a standard machine.

Two constraints bind harder than they first appear. First, you must fully train your own algorithms — pretrained systems trained on outside data are permitted only as a comparison baseline, never as a submitted model. Second, for HD and DI grades the specification explicitly requires that you investigate how the state of the data affects results, propose improvements, implement them, and wrap the result in a GUI or API so a customer is not typing at a terminal.

Independent evaluation is also mandatory, and it means two distinct things in the spec: compare your numbers against published work with the same goals, and evaluate on data held completely outside the scope of your training and tuning.

The key aspect of this assignment isn’t your code or model, but the thought process behind your work. Assignment specification, §3.4

What protected data preparation proves
  1. Twenty-four article types are weak but still official

    The training-fitted output space has 124 article types. The main comparison slice has 100 with at least three independent training families. The other 24 stay in model and submission outputs and are reported separately.

  2. “NA” is a real usage label

    The training partition contains 50 literal NA usage labels and one real blank. Reads use keep_default_na=False, so those cases stay different.

  3. Both image sizes are one product

    Every train, validation, and holdout ID has one original and one high-resolution image. They share an ID and group and their weights sum to one. Prediction IDs and quarantine are excluded.

  4. Validation rows do not equal independent evidence

    All 100 train-supported article types appear in validation, but 18 have only one validation family and 26 have fewer than three. One usage class has two validation families. Holdout target coverage is not inspected. Thin classes need wide uncertainty, even when product-row coverage says 100%.

  5. Family blocking buys safety by reducing independence

    The 38,551 active product rows become 27,009 family units: 11,542 fewer units, or 29.9%. There are 4,567 multi-row families and the largest has 80 rows. Exact hashes, accepted visual links, and normalized names define the block basis. The name rule can over-block different designs, so family sizes and bases stay visible.

  6. The support threshold is a measured trade-off

    Requiring 2, 3, 4, or 5 training families keeps 105, 100, 98, or 94 article types. Three is the chosen balance between broader output coverage and credible independent support. It is not a claim that rare official labels are invalid.

  7. Image quality evidence stays train only

    A fixed 2,048-product training sample measures brightness, contrast, sharpness, and foreground/background scale. Paired train-only RGB mean and standard deviation set normalization. These values define augmentation bounds and later error slices; they do not claim a model gain.

04
The pipeline

One split, one training loop, one metrics module

The shape of the system, and the four contracts that keep thirty-plus experiments comparable to one another. Circled numbers on the diagram mark each contract.

1

data/processed/splits.csv

Written exactly once; read by every notebook, the search index and the app. No other file may call train_test_split. It is what makes cross-model comparison controlled rather than coincidental, and what keeps the held-out portion genuinely independent.

2

results/runs.csv

Appended by code on every run — never by hand. One row per experiment with hyperparameters, metrics, training time and parameter count. The report’s comparison tables are a groupby over this file, not archaeology through old output cells.

3

train/loop.py

Every experiment trains through the same function with the same early stopping and logging. Without it, “CNN versus tuned CNN” secretly compares two training regimes instead of two models.

4

eval/metrics.py

One implementation of each task owner’s frozen metric definitions, class breakdowns and confusion matrices — so no task can quietly change a denominator or scoring rule.

07
Open questions

One data-preparation decision closed; two model questions remain

The data policy is now evidence-backed. The remaining choices need registered model results or a small later human validation, not an opinion from a meeting.

Accepted · data-preparation evidence

What happens to the rare articleType classes?

Keep every image-backed label in official model and submission outputs. Classes with fewer than three independent product families stay trainable but leave the primary cross-model supported comparison. No semantic merge is made.

Evidence: 124 train-observed outputs; 100 have three-family training support. Train-only thresholds 2/3/4/5 support 105/100/98/94 classes.

Open · needs model evidence

One backbone with two heads, or two separate models?

The submission format permits either. Multi-task learning is the stronger claim to going beyond class material, and gender and usage plausibly share features — but shared training can let the 76.7% Casual majority drown the smaller target.

Evidence needed: each target’s frozen primary and supporting metrics, both ways, on the same split and budget.

Open · non-blocking human validation

What does “similar” mean for Task 4?

No ground-truth similarity labels exist. A metadata proxy — same articleType and baseColour — is measurable but circular. It is a metadata proxy, never real-world similarity ground truth.

Evidence needed later: agreement between the proxy and human judgement on the IDs listed in docs/reviews/open_decisions.md. This does not block data preparation.
