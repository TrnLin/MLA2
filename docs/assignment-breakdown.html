01
The brief

The graded artefact is the reasoning, not the accuracy

What the specification asks for, and the one sentence in it that should shape every decision you make.

Fashion e-commerce platforms use machine learning to organise and search catalogues far too large to tag by hand. Given a product photograph, the system should identify the item, infer who and when it is for, and surface visually similar products. That is the problem this assignment simulates, using a catalogue of roughly 38.6 thousand training images and 5.8 thousand unlabelled test images.

The deliverables are four: a report of no more than five pages plus two pages of appendices; at least four trained models with the scripts that run them; a set of predictions produced by your ultimate judgement; and the notebooks or scripts that built everything, with a README so a marker can reproduce it on a standard machine.

Two constraints bind harder than they first appear. First, you must fully train your own algorithms — pretrained systems trained on outside data are permitted only as a comparison baseline, never as a submitted model. Second, for HD and DI grades the specification explicitly requires that you investigate how the state of the data affects results, propose improvements, implement them, and wrap the result in a GUI or API so a customer is not typing at a terminal.

Independent evaluation is also mandatory, and it means two distinct things in the spec: compare your numbers against published work with the same goals, and evaluate on data held completely outside the scope of your training and tuning.

The key aspect of this assignment isn’t your code or model, but the thought process behind your work. Assignment specification, §3.4

Traps found by measuring, not reading
  1. “NA” is a string, not a gap

    usage holds the literal text NA in 71 rows, and is genuinely empty in exactly 1. A default read_csv collapses both into NaN and quietly destroys the distinction.

  2. Five labels point at nothing

    Ids 12347, 39401, 39403, 39410 and 39425 exist in the CSV with no matching .jpg. That is why 38,617 rows yield only 38,612 usable examples.

  3. Seven classes have one example

    Shoe Laces, Suits, Ties and Cufflinks, Cushion Covers, Body Wash and Scrub, Ipad and Lounge Tshirts appear once each. A single example cannot be split into train and validation, so any policy you pick must be defended in the report.

  4. The header has two phantom columns

    styles_train.csv ends its header with ,, — twelve fields for ten names. Parsers must tolerate it rather than shift every column by two.

  5. Majority-class accuracy is already high

    Predicting the single most common label scores 76.8% on usage, 54.2% on gender and 49.6% on season. Accuracy is therefore close to useless here; macro-F1 is the honest measure.

  6. Not everything is clothing

    masterCategory includes Personal Care (1,001 rows — fragrance, nail polish), Free Items (83), Sporting Goods (25) and a single Home item. “Season” and “gender” mean something different for a perfume than for a coat.

  7. 60×80 is the whole budget

    Every image is 4,800 pixels. Upscaling beyond roughly 96 px invents no new detail and only buys compute cost — but small inputs also mean fine texture cues, which is what distinguishes Formal from Casual, may simply not be present.

  8. The submission format is frozen

    styles_prediction.csv has header id,gender,articleType,season,usage over 5,829 blank rows. Four separate columns is precisely why Task 3 is treated as two targets rather than one combined class.

04
The pipeline

One split, one training loop, one metrics module

The shape of the system, and the four contracts that keep thirty-plus experiments comparable to one another. Circled numbers on the diagram mark each contract.

1

data/processed/splits.csv

Written exactly once; read by every notebook, the search index and the app. No other file may call train_test_split. It is what makes cross-model comparison controlled rather than coincidental, and what keeps the held-out portion genuinely independent.

2

results/runs.csv

Appended by code on every run — never by hand. One row per experiment with hyperparameters, metrics, training time and parameter count. The report’s comparison tables are a groupby over this file, not archaeology through old output cells.

3

train/loop.py

Every experiment trains through the same function with the same early stopping and logging. Without it, “CNN versus tuned CNN” secretly compares two training regimes instead of two models.

4

eval/metrics.py

One implementation of macro-F1, per-class breakdown and confusion matrices for all four targets — so no task can quietly report accuracy while another reports macro-F1.

07
Open questions

Three decisions waiting on evidence

These are deliberately unresolved. Each needs a number from the EDA notebook, not an opinion from a meeting — and each is exactly the kind of choice the Approach criterion asks you to justify.

Open · needs EDA

What happens to the rare articleType classes?

Drop them, merge them upward through subCategory, or keep them and report the failure honestly. Each choice changes what macro-F1 even means, so the report must state which was taken and why.

Evidence needed: per-class counts, and macro-F1 under each policy.

Open · needs EDA

One backbone with two heads, or two separate models?

The submission format permits either. Multi-task learning is the stronger claim to going beyond class material, and gender and usage plausibly share features — but shared training can let the 76.8% Casual majority drown the smaller target.

Evidence needed: per-target macro-F1, both ways, on the same split.

Open · needs EDA

What does “similar” mean for Task 4?

No ground-truth similarity labels exist. A metadata proxy — same articleType and baseColour, say — is measurable but circular; human inspection is honest but small. Probably both, reported separately.

Evidence needed: agreement between the proxy and human judgement on a sample.
