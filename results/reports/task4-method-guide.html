1. The whole search pipeline

Every method has the same job: turn a picture into numbers that are easy to compare. Only the middle description method changes.

Raw phototeacher, V1, or user upload
Prepare itRGB, resize and white letterbox to 240×320
Describe itR5, VICReg model, HOG, HSV-edge, or fusion
Compareexact cosine similarity against the gallery
Return Top 10ten distinct product IDs
Important size rule. Raw teacher files begin at 60×80. The model does not train on that raw size. Every Task 4 method receives the frozen 240×320 prepared image.

2. Embedding and ResNet

Word meaning

Embedding

An embedding is a short list of numbers that describes a picture. Our learned models make 128 numbers. Similar products should make similar lists.

Think of it like placing every product on a large map. Similar clothes should stand close together.

Model part

ResNet encoder

A ResNet is a convolutional neural network, or CNN. It learns small visual detectors such as edges first, then larger patterns such as sleeves, collars, shapes, and textures.

The encoder is the part that changes a 240×320 image into the compact description. R1, R3, R4 and B1 add a projection head; R5 uses a 128-number bottleneck.

3. VICReg — used by R1, R2, R3, R4 and B1

VICReg learns without category labels. We show it two pictures of the same product: the teacher view and the V1 view. It follows three rules at once.

1. Invariance: pull together

Make the two views of the same product produce similar numbers.

2. Variance: do not collapse

Do not give every picture the same answer. Different products must spread out.

3. Covariance: do not repeat yourself

Do not let all 128 numbers copy one another. Each number should add information.

Why we used it

Every development product already has a teacher/V1 pair. VICReg can learn that both sources show the same item without saying every other fashion item is wrong.

What it can miss

It mainly learns “these two files are the same product.” That does not directly teach which different products a person considers similar. A clean teacher/V1 pair may also be too easy.

VICReg loss = 25 × match-pair loss + 25 × spread-out loss + 1 × no-copy loss

4. R3 geometry training

R3 still uses VICReg. Its one main change is what one picture in the pair looks like during training.

Same product pairteacher + V1
Keep one cleannormal prepared image
Change onecrop, scale, move, wide or tall canvas
VICReglearn that both are still the same product

Strength

It teaches the exact failure we measured. R3 keeps a tall-canvas nDCG@10 of about 0.301, very close to its clean 0.311.

Weakness

Its normal average quality is far below R5 and the HOG + HSV-edge fusion. Robust does not automatically mean accurate.

5. Triplet loss — added by R4

Triplet loss uses three products at a time.

Anchor

The product we start from.

Positive

A different product from the same product-family group. Pull it closer.

Negative

A product from another family. Push it farther away.

It sounds closer to what shoppers want: similar products, not duplicate files. But only about 41.9% of fold-1 training rows have a valid family positive, and the family groups mostly come from cleaned product names. That signal is incomplete and noisy. R4 scored slightly below R3, so this extra lesson did not help here.

6. Autoencoder — used by R5

An autoencoder learns by playing “squash and redraw.” It does not use category labels or teacher/V1 matching pairs.

240×320 phototeacher or V1 as a separate example
Encoderscratch ResNet-18 compresses the photo
128-number bottleneckthe only information allowed through
Decoder redrawscompare redraw with the original content pixels

Why exactly 128 numbers?

All learned candidates must return the same size description. The decoder has no shortcut around the bottleneck, so it must rebuild the image using only those 128 numbers. After training, we discard the redraw and use the bottleneck for search.

Strength

R5 reached 0.50289, the best learned score. It preserved far more useful visual detail than the VICReg candidates.

Weakness

Redrawing rewards pixel layout. R5 can remember where an item sits instead of only what it is. Its score falls badly when the white canvas changes. Training is also slower and the decoder makes the checkpoint larger.

7. HOG — Histogram of Oriented Gradients

HOG is hand-written maths, not a learned model. It asks where edges are and which way they point.

Make luminanceturn RGB into Rec.709 brightness
Find gradientsmeasure edge strength and direction
Count directions8 bins inside 32×32 cells
Normalise blocks2×2 cells, one-cell stride, L2-Hys

The final HOG description has 1,728 numbers. It ignores colour. It is content-mask aware, so preprocessing padding does not become a fake edge.

Strength

Fast, explainable, no GPU training, and good source robustness: 0.97165.

Weakness

Its grid is tied to edge location. Move or shrink the clothing and the edges land in different cells. Clean V1 nDCG@10 0.467 falls to 0.017 on the wide-canvas test.

How this relates to the HOG recommendation article

The article also makes HOG vectors and uses brute-force nearest neighbours to return similar fashion images. We use that core idea. We do not copy its new train_test_split or its scaler fitted over all features, because our one approved split is data/processed/splits.csv and validation information must not leak into fitting.

8. Spatial HSV-edge

This is another fixed, untrained description. It keeps colour and rough location.

HSV colour

Hue means the colour family, such as red or blue. Saturation means strong or grey-looking colour. Value means brightness. HSV separates these ideas more clearly than raw red, green and blue values.

Spatial edge

The image is divided into a 4×4 grid. Each area records colour and edge information. Keeping the grid means “blue near the top” is different from “blue near the bottom.”

All parts form one 400-number descriptor. It scores strongly at 0.48988, but colour and spatial location make it more sensitive to source changes and canvas changes.

9. Equal HOG + HSV-edge fusion

Fusion joins HOG shape information with HSV-edge colour and layout information. It still has zero learned weights.

fused = [HOG × 1/√2, HSV-edge × 1/√2]
Each part is first normalised to length 1. The equal weights make each whole block contribute half of the final cosine similarity, even though HOG has more numbers.

Why it works

HOG supplies shape. HSV-edge supplies colour and rough layout. Their mistakes are not identical, so one can add information the other missed. The fusion reaches 0.50267, only 0.00022 below R5.

What it still cannot fix

Both parts use fixed spatial grids. The fusion still falls from clean 0.50025 to wide 0.03295 and tall 0.17080. Its 2,128-value index is also much larger than a learned 128-value index.

10. Cosine search, Top-K and nDCG@10

Cosine similarity

Imagine each number list as an arrow. Cosine similarity asks whether two arrows point in the same direction. Same direction means more similar. Because our vectors have length 1, size cannot cheat the comparison.

Top-K

Sort every gallery product from most similar to least similar and keep the first K. For Task 4, K is usually 10. We collapse duplicate views so one product can occupy only one result slot.

nDCG@10

A score for the quality and order of the first 10 results. A useful match near position 1 earns more than the same match near position 10. 1.0 is perfect; 0.0 is useless.

12. Small glossary

Augmentation
A changed training copy, such as moving or resizing the clothing, used to teach robustness.
Bottleneck
A deliberately small information path. R5 must squeeze the whole picture through 128 numbers.
Canvas
The white area around the product inside the photo. This is not an HTML canvas.
Decoder
The R5 part that tries to redraw the image from the bottleneck.
Descriptor
A list of numbers made by fixed maths, such as HOG or HSV-edge.
Encoder
A model part that turns an image into a compact number list.
Fold
One of five fixed data parts. A different fold is held out during each stability run.
From scratch
Every trainable weight starts without pretrained knowledge.
Gallery
The stored product images or embeddings that a query is searched against.
L2 normalisation
Rescale a vector so its total length is 1 before cosine search.
MSE
Mean squared error: R5's average squared pixel difference between its redraw and the original.
Source
Where a product view came from. This project compares teacher and V1 sources.
